Core promise
One meal.

Design principles
  1. Food before numbers — cups and ounces lead, macros follow.
  2. Difference without judgement — no portion looks better.
  3. Motion explains relationships, never decorates.
  4. Spacious by default; density is an opt-in.

04b / Direction 3 — accent alternatives

Replacing the clay accent

The accent does one heavy job in this product: it identifies the second household member next to a green-coded first member. Each option below is shown on the same Direction 3 components — profile pill, portion block, favourite state and tag — against primary green 17794F.

Deeper and more grown-up; carries white text at 4.6:1. Risk: sits close to the error red, so error states must move to a cooler crimson.

Dried plum

Highest separation from green in every colour-vision simulation, and it never collides with success, warning or error. Risk: less “food” and slightly cosmetic on large fills.
Slate blue
4A7A96
C6 oz P4 oz
Crispest and most product-like — a natural fit for Direction 3. Risk: it is the coolest option and it competes with the information blue.
Recommendation
Take terracotta C25E52 as Direction 3’s accent, and move error to a cooler A32E33 so the two never blur.
It keeps the warmth that stops Direction 3 reading clinical, holds white text at button size, and is clearly separable from green without going cosmetic. Dried plum is the fallback if colour-vision testing shows terracotta and green are still being confused.
Rules that apply whichever we pick
  • The accent identifies a person, never a state — it is banned from primary buttons, links and alerts.
  • The accent pill always carries the initial and a dashed ring; green always carries a solid ring.
  • Whatever we choose must clear 3:1 against white as a fill and 4.5:1 as text at its 700 step.
  • A third and fourth household member extend into plum then slate — so the accent choice sets the whole profile ramp.

06 / Direction chosen

Direction 3, with terracotta in place of clay

Modern premium product carries a seven-day plan, a portion comparison and a 24-item grocery list without crowding — the density the product actually needs. Terracotta and a warm-cast surface keep it from reading clinical, which is the one real risk of this direction.

Sections 07 to 20 are now built in this direction end to end.

Borrow from direction 1
Card padding at 20/24, the calm amount of whitespace between sections, and the restraint on how much colour any one screen carries.
Borrow from direction 2
The mono eyebrow labels and the generous 3:2 hero ratio on meal detail, so recipe pages still feel like food and not like a dashboard.
Test with users
Whether the portion comparison reads as neutral rather than competitive; whether terracotta and green stay distinguishable for colour-blind users (plum is the fallback); whether the tighter density still feels calm on a phone.

20 / Accessibility and responsive rules

Accessibility rules

✓Body text is 4.5:1 minimum; secondary text never lighter than 55605A on cream.
✓Focus is a 3px primary-500 halo at 2px offset — visible on every interactive element, never removed on mouse users.
✓Touch targets are 44×44 minimum, with 8px between adjacent targets.
✓Every field has a persistent visible label; placeholders are examples, never labels.
✓Errors sit under the field, are tied via aria-describedby, carry an icon, and say how to fix it.
✓Selection always carries a non-colour cue: a check glyph, a 1.5px border, or a weight change.
✓Portion comparison labels every quantity in words and initials; the green/terracotta pair is a reinforcement, not the message.
✓Disabled controls keep 3:1 contrast and always explain why nearby — never a dead grey button with no reason.
✓Mobile body text is 16px minimum; numerals never below 15px.
✓prefers-reduced-motion replaces movement with cross-fades under 120ms; nothing loops.
